Last Tuesday, a queue appeared in Little Wumbleton, and nobody knew what for.
It began at nine, with one man named Derek.
By noon, four hundred people stood behind him, politely.
They brought flasks, folding chairs, and a brass band.
Wait, what were they all waiting for?
Nobody knew, and nobody asked, because that would be rude.
You see, Derek had only stopped to tie his shoelace, and everyone behind him assumed he was first.
Ever joined a queue just because it was long? Everyone does.
The vicar blessed it, the mayor sold tea, and by dusk it had its own flag.
Derek, meanwhile, walked home.
