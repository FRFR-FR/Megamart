# "The Tower That Was Saved By War" — Leaning Tower of Pisa, Italy (30 s)
Format: vertical story short, ~30 s, one steady British male narrator, no captions.
Facts: Foundations laid 1173, tower began sinking after the second floor in 1178, work paused for decades of war until 1233, foundation only about 3 m deep in soft soil, finished 1372, tilt 5.5 degrees in 1990, 70 tonnes of soil removed (Wikipedia, Leaning Tower of Pisa).

Pisa began a bell tower in eleven seventy-three, and within five years it leaned.
Its foundation was just three metres deep, in soft clay.
Then war broke out, and for fifty-five years, nobody added a single brick.
Builders returned, built one side taller, and bent the tower into a banana.
Wait, why didn't it just topple over?
You see, those quiet war years let the clay slowly harden.
Ever stacked books on a squishy sofa? It sinks, then settles.
By nineteen ninety it leaned five and a half degrees, so engineers removed seventy tonnes of soil.
Today it still leans, and every tourist pretends to hold it up.
