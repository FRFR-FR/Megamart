# "The Bridge That Wobbled" — Millennium Bridge, London (30 s)
Format: vertical story short, ~30 s, one steady British male narrator, original story and characters.
Facts: Opened 10 June 2000; about 90,000 people crossed, up to 2,000 on it at once; closed 12 June 2000; pedestrians unconsciously matched their steps to the sway (synchronous lateral excitation); fixed with 37 viscous dampers + 52 tuned mass dampers; reopened 22 February 2002; nicknamed the Wobbly Bridge. (Wikipedia, Millennium Bridge, London)

London opened a bridge in two thousand, and within hours it was swaying sideways.
Ninety thousand people crossed that weekend, two thousand at a time.
They giggled, grabbed the rails, and walked wider, like sailors.
Wait, why would a steel bridge wobble just because people walked?
Two days later, they shut it.
You see, when the deck moved, everyone stepped sideways to balance, all at the same time.
Ever stood on a braking bus? You lean without thinking.
Their steps pushed the bridge, it pushed back, and the wobble wouldn't stop.
Engineers fitted eighty-nine dampers, and it reopened in two thousand and two.
Londoners still call it the Wobbly Bridge.
