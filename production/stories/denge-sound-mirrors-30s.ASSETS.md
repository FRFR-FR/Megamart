# Denge Sound Mirrors — scene and production notes

## Story
A sourced explainer about the experimental acoustic aircraft-warning station at Denge, on the Kent coast. The short uses the package's existing articulated character rig, inked concrete primitives, coastal props, and camera format.

## Shot-to-narration plan
- 0.0–2.4s: Front view of the listening operator, with the concrete installation behind him
- 2.4–6.3s: Wide reveal of the compact Kent coast test site
- 6.3–7.35s: First design — the 20-foot slab
- 7.35–8.4s: Second design — the 30-foot concave dish
- 8.4–9.5s: Third design — the long 200-foot strip mirror
- 9.5–12.4s: Operator at the listening trumpet
- 12.4–15.5s: The open sea and the claimed listening range
- 15.5–17.9s: Elevated view linking two listening stations along the coast
- 17.9–19.5s: Punch-in on the curved concrete face for the question
- 19.5–23.0s: Camera glides from the reflecting surface toward its focal point
- 23.0–26.6s: Operator cups his hands, matching the everyday analogy
- 26.6–28.8s: Lighthouse and sea at golden hour as radar replaces the mirrors
- 28.8–30.0s: Wide closing view of the three mirror forms

## Asset constraints
All scene elements use existing types and builders from `factory/render/index.html` and `factory/render/lib/props.js`. The three mirror forms are approximated with the repository's standard inked concrete box primitives; no renderer code, camera logic, imported stock footage, labels, captions, or generated image assets are introduced. The environment remains one compact coast-side site.

## Sources
- Kent County Council Historic Environment Record, “Acoustic sound mirrors at Greatstone-on-Sea, Lydd”: 1927–1930 design sequence; 20-foot slab, 30-foot bowl, 200-foot strip; trumpet/stethoscope listening; reported 8–24-mile range.
- RAF Air Defence Radar Museum: context for acoustic mirrors as a pre-radar aircraft-detection system.
