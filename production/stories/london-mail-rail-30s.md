# Britain's Hidden Mail Railway — London (30 seconds)
Format: 30-second vertical cartoon-3D story short, one British male narrator, no captions or labels.
Facts: London's Mail Rail opened in 1927; the Post Office built it to move mail beneath the streets between sorting offices; its electric trains ran through narrow tunnels and the railway eventually closed in 2003 (The Postal Museum, “Mail Rail”).

Britain once hid a railway beneath London — and it carried millions of letters.
The Post Office opened Mail Rail in 1927 to move mail beneath the streets.
Electric trains raced between sorting offices while traffic crawled above them.
Look at the workers. Parcels were loaded, sorted, and pushed straight onto the trains.
The tunnels were tiny, with some sections barely wide enough for the little railway.
You see, the whole system was built around speed: move the mail underground, avoid the roads, repeat.
Ever imagine a postman sending your letter through a secret subway? That was basically the idea.
For decades, London carried on above while millions of letters travelled underneath.
The hidden railway finally closed in 2003.
A complete railway under London — hidden in plain sight.
