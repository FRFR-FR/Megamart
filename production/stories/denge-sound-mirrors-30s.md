# "Britain's Giant Concrete Ears" — Denge Sound Mirrors, Kent (30 seconds)
Format: 30-second vertical cartoon-3D story short, one steady British male narrator, no captions or labels.
Facts: Denge's experimental sound mirrors were built between 1927 and 1930: a 20-foot slab, a 30-foot bowl, and a 200-foot strip; operators focused sound with a metal trumpet and listened through a stethoscope; in good conditions, aircraft could be heard 8–24 miles away; radar made the mirrors obsolete by 1935 (Kent County Council Historic Environment Record, Acoustic sound mirrors at Greatstone-on-Sea, Lydd; RAF Air Defence Radar Museum).

Britain built giant concrete ears to detect enemy aircraft.
At Denge in Kent, engineers tested three designs from nineteen twenty-seven to nineteen thirty.
A twenty-foot slab became a thirty-foot dish, then a two-hundred-foot wall.
Operators aimed metal trumpets at the noise and listened through stethoscopes.
In good conditions, they could hear aircraft up to twenty-four miles away.
Two stations helped pinpoint a plane's position and direction.
Wait, how can concrete hear?
You see, the curved surface gathered sound waves and focused them onto one point.
Ever cup your hands around your ears to catch a whisper? Same trick, enormous.
Radar made the mirrors obsolete by nineteen thirty-five.
Britain's giant ears had heard enough.
