# "Britain’s Giant Concrete Ears" — Denge Sound Mirrors, Kent (30 seconds)
Format: 30-second vertical cartoon-3D story short, one British male narrator, no captions or labels.
Facts: at Denge, the acoustic mirrors were built from 1927 to 1930: a 20-foot slab, 30-foot bowl and 200-foot wall; operators focused sound through a metal trumpet and stethoscope; in favourable conditions aircraft could reportedly be heard up to 24 miles away; radar made them obsolete by 1935 (Kent County Council Historic Environment Record, “Acoustic Sound Mirrors at Greatstone-on-Sea, Lydd”).

Before radar, Britain tried to hear enemy planes with concrete.
At Denge in Kent, RAF engineers built three giant listening mirrors.
First came a twenty-foot slab, then a thirty-foot bowl, then a two-hundred-foot wall.
Crews aimed metal trumpets at distant engines and listened through stethoscopes.
In good conditions, they could hear aircraft up to twenty-four miles away.
Two stations compared the sound's direction to work out where the aircraft was.
Wait, how can a wall hear anything?
You see, each curved surface gathered sound waves and focused them onto a listening point.
Ever cup your hands around your ears to catch a whisper? Same trick, enormous.
Then radar arrived and made the concrete ears obsolete by nineteen thirty-five.
Britain's strangest early-warning system was built to listen.
